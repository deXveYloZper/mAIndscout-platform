## AL-00 — Retracted: "protected-context" claim suppression

**Proposed:** treating candidate disclosures like parental-leave history as requiring the same hard extraction-level suppression as GDPR special-category data.
**Retracted because:** no concrete pathway to harm existed. The outward projection (E-i12) never draws from personal narrative; scoring features have no slot for causal career-event narrative; and the closed claim-type registry (Feature 3) means such content was never going to become a claim in the first place, extractors only produce instances of registered types. The existing architecture already fully contained this without any new mechanism. Recorded as a calibration data point: apply the "is there an actual pathway" test *before* proposing a safeguard, not after.

## AL-01 — Retracted: sourcing discovery treated as requiring special compliance caution

**Proposed:** that identifying and evaluating a real, named individual surfaced by an ordinary web/LinkedIn search should be avoided in testing or development contexts pending some form of sandboxing.
**Retracted because:** this is ordinary, lawful, universal recruiting practice — indistinguishable from what any recruiter does by hand — and treating it as requiring extra caution was inconsistent with how every other piece of real personal data in this project has already been handled. The real gates are downstream of discovery: what happens once a lead is stored, retained, and contacted (AL-28, AL-30) — not the act of finding and evaluating public professional information itself.

## Ingestion (amends 02-Feature-1, schema in 01 §D1)

**AL-02.** Job descriptions need staleness detection. A JD can embed hard process dates (interview/hackathon dates, a stated start window) that elapse before the document is processed. Add a `JobIntelligence`-side check: when embedded process dates have passed relative to `as_of`/processing time, raise a Critic-equivalent flag — *"this posting's own timeline has passed; confirm still open before submitting anyone."* This is the JD-side analog of a CV's `as_of` uncertainty; JDs get their own version because their staleness signal is different in kind (hard dates, not vague "Present" tokens).

**AL-03.** Extend the existing "hiring company from content, never branding" rule explicitly to cover filenames. A file named `Senior_AI_Engineer_-_Oplane.pdf` whose body text never names "Oplane" must not have that name treated as authoritative — filenames are, at most, a weak search hint for a human reviewer, never a resolution source.

**AL-04.** Transcript-specific entity normalization. Auto-transcribed call recordings reliably mis-hear proper nouns multiple different ways *within the same document* (observed twice independently: "Delbridge Solutions" also transcribed as "Telbase"; "CarbonQuota" also as "Carbon Cutter," "CarbonCo," "Carver quota"). Before company/entity resolution runs on transcript-derived text, cluster phonetically/orthographically similar candidate spellings within the document as probable single-entity variants. This is intra-document noise, distinct from and prior to the existing cross-document alias handling (Facebook/Meta-style).

**AL-05.** Job-level resolution (01 §D1, dispatch) must handle a transcript preceding its JD, not just JD-vs-JD deduplication. An operator call can originate a role's requirements before any formal JD document exists (observed directly: "I still need to put together the JD properly to send over to you," said *after* the substantive requirements discussion). The resolution match (hiring company + normalized title + recency) must treat transcript-then-JD as an ordinary case, with the JD arriving as later formalization/supersession of transcript-derived requirement claims.

## Identity & Company Resolution (amends 02-Feature-2, schema in 01 §D3)

**AL-06.** `registry_ids` and provider routing must be jurisdiction-aware. As specified, `registry_ids` implicitly defaults to UK/US registries (Companies House, Crunchbase); a real Canadian company (Vena Solutions) has no natural home in that shape. Change `registry_ids` to a jurisdiction-keyed map (e.g. `{"CA-federal": {...}, "AT-firmenbuch": {...}, "DE-handelsregister": {...}}`), and add a jurisdiction → registry-provider routing table to the Research Gateway's provider configuration. A `null` under a UK-only assumption looks like "checked, not found" when the true state is "wrong registry entirely" — this must not happen silently.

**AL-07.** A company's registered/incorporation address is never evidence of its actual hiring footprint. It confirms the legal entity exists; it says nothing about where employees work. This must be stated explicitly wherever geographic plausibility (01 §F) is implemented, since it's an easy and consequential conflation.

**AL-08.** New claim type: `CompanyWorkPolicyClaim` — payload `{policy: remote_friendly | hybrid | onsite_only | distributed_countries[]}`, time-bounded like other company claims, researched once per company and cached. This directly answers "does this company hire remotely" as a stored, reusable fact rather than a judgment the geographic plausibility check re-derives, informally, from research context each time a mismatched candidate happens to come up. Add to the "Company (time-bounded)" claim group in 01 §F.

## Claims Engine (amends 02-Feature-3, schema in 01 §D2)

**AL-09.** Concurrency plausibility flags must be written symmetrically to both overlapping claims, cross-referencing each other, not to only one side of the pair. A reviewer must see the context regardless of which of the two claims they open first.

**AL-10.** `evidence.origin` (01 §D2 / B2) needs a fifth value: `relayed`. Covers facts an agency conveys about a candidate's own situation — their title, as told to a recruiter, appearing in screening notes — which is neither `employer` (the employer didn't say it) nor safely `candidate` (it's one hop removed via a third party). `relayed` must never corroborate with `candidate`-origin evidence for the same fact, or an agency's paraphrase of a candidate's self-report would falsely double-count against the candidate's own CV.

**AL-11.** `CompensationExpectationClaim` needs a candidate-scope/pair-scope split, mirroring the existing candidate-scope/pair-scope split already used for Brief items. A candidate's standing general expectation (e.g. "£65K ask") is a different fact from what they negotiated and accepted for one specific, compelling opportunity (e.g. "£60K accepted here, given equity and role"). Collapsing these loses information the next, less compelling opportunity needs.

**AL-12.** New claim type: `CompetingProcessClaim` — payload `{type: internal_promotion | external_offer | other_agency_pipeline, status, disclosed_via}`. Covers a competing process disclosed by the candidate that is entirely outside our own tracked pipelines (e.g. an internal promotion interview at their current employer) — distinct from the existing `competing_offer` flag, which only fires from a pair we ourselves track reaching `offer`. Feeds the same urgency signal into the Brief and pipeline pacing.

## Cross-cutting infrastructure (amends 01 §D6, referenced throughout 02)

**AL-13.** `flag_type_registry` — the single highest-priority item in this log. Every flag key used anywhere in the system (`concurrency.overlap_with`, `extraordinary_unverified`, `possible_duplicate_stint`, `single_source_web`, `suppressed_by_critic`, `geographic.severity`, `possible_deal_breaker`, `competing_offer`, `no_longer_meets`, `insufficient_identity`, `budget_truncated`, and the two new ones in this log, `possible_dual_representation` and any future additions) must be a registered entry: `(key, default_severity, brief_template, blocks_auto_approval: bool, blocks_matching: bool, valid_subject_types)`, validated on every write. This is a structural gap: the Screening Brief (Feature 9) is designed as a deterministic compiler over a known flag vocabulary, and no such registered vocabulary currently exists in the schema — flags are presently just strings any agent can invent, with no contract linking a new flag to Brief behavior.

## Multi-Agent Intelligence / Job Intelligence (amends 02-Feature-5)

**AL-14.** Operator call transcripts are legitimate `JobIntelligence` input, not only `CandidateIntelligence` input. A transcript can be the *first* evidence about a job's requirements, and can add job-context detail (growth path, engagement model, comparable-role positioning) that the eventual JD text never states. Dispatch (02-Feature-1, step 7) must fan a single transcript out to multiple pipelines when `document_subject` links it to subjects of different types (a candidate *and* a job/company) simultaneously — the schema already supports this via per-row `subject_type`; only the dispatch logic needs updating to act on it.

**AL-15.** New flag: `possible_dual_representation`. When an ingested document (an ATS/CRM export, or equivalent) reveals a candidate was already tracked in another recruiting pipeline as of a recent date, this must surface as a Decision before any outreach — *"this record shows prior/possible concurrent engagement with another recruiting pipeline; confirm representation status before contacting."* Register in `flag_type_registry` per AL-13, `blocks_auto_approval: false, blocks_matching: false`, routes to the Screening Brief.

**AL-16.** Document taxonomy (01 §D1) needs an `ats_export` / `third_party_system_export` type — a document containing candidate content bundled with operational metadata (named actors, pipeline/tag references) from a different system. Named actors appearing only in metadata/log sections (not the narrative body) must never become candidate entities or claim subjects — the same subject-attribution discipline as agency-contact-detail bleed (Feature 2), applied to a document type the original taxonomy had no slot for.

**AL-17.** Document taxonomy also needs a multi-subject splitter path exercised for shortlist-style PDFs and email threads that bundle several people into one file — confirmed as a real gap (no test case in this project has yet included a genuine multi-person single document; the splitter described in 02-Feature-1.6 has design but no exercised golden-set case).

## Scoring (amends 02-Feature-7)

**AL-18.** `score.breakdown` is formalized as a concrete structure, not merely described as "explainable." Per requirement: `{requirement_id, requirement_text, strength, outcome, contributing_claims: [claim_id...], weight, points_contributed}`. This is what makes explainability enforceable — every point in a score traces to specific claim IDs, reproducibly.

**AL-19.** UNKNOWN excludes its own weight from the scoring denominator at the *per-requirement* level (`points_contributed: null`, weight excluded from the sum used for normalization), mirroring the existing per-dimension renormalization rule in General Strength (H8b). Without this, an UNKNOWN silently behaves like a zero and drags the composite down exactly like the bug already fixed at the dimension level — just one level lower, and previously unstated there.

**AL-20.** New signal: `exceeds_expectations`, distinct from `strong`. A requirement cleared by a wide margin (e.g. a 13-year AI lead applying to an "all levels" seat) is not a gap and not undifferentiated strength — it's a distinct retention/expectations-mismatch risk. Routes to the Brief as a question ("confirm scope, autonomy, and comp expectations align") — probe, don't punish, applied to the *top* of the fit distribution rather than only the bottom.

**AL-21.** Compensation parsing must be locale-aware. Salary quoting convention (monthly vs. annual) varies by country — Sweden commonly quotes monthly gross; UK/US/Canada quote annual. A naive default produces a wildly wrong number (misreading "70,000 SEK/month" as annual understates real compensation by roughly 12x). `CompensationExpectationClaim` and job-side compensation extraction need country-of-posting-aware parsing before international expansion, not after a bad match ships.

## Matching & Jobs (amends 02-Feature-8, schema in 01 §D5)

**AL-22.** Location/visa `RequirementClaim` extraction must capture three separate facts, not one collapsed filter: sponsorship required (y/n), relocation assistance offered (y/n), and stated current-residence requirement. A posting saying "based in Germany or the UK, no visa support or relocation assistance" is not equivalent to "must currently live in Germany or the UK" — an EU citizen elsewhere in the EU may be eligible and willing to self-relocate. Collapsing these risks silently excluding eligible, willing candidates. Residence-mismatch-but-eligible routes to the Brief, never to auto-exclusion.

**AL-23.** Agency-anonymized hiring companies need an explicit, first-class job state: `hiring_company_id: null` with `flags: {client_identity: "anonymized_by_agency"}`. This is not an error state or a resolution failure — it is a legitimate, common, ethical agency practice (protecting a client from direct approach) that the system must represent correctly rather than guess around. Candidates can be matched and screened against such a job; they cannot be submitted until a fuller document (a transcript, a later disclosure) resolves `hiring_company_id` — confirmed directly in testing, where an anonymized Propel-branded JD's client was later named as "Delbridge Solutions" in an unrelated call transcript, exactly the resolution path this state anticipates.

## Business Development — client-known expansion (amends 02-Feature-11)

**AL-24.** Career-page-monitoring priority (the signal that a candidate's current/past employer is worth watching for new roles) must decay when the underlying stint closes (`valid_to` set), and must weight *current* employer more heavily than *past* employer — a live connection is a materially stronger discovery signal than a historical one.

**AL-25.** The same monitoring priority must be weighted by company size/stage, favoring small/growth-stage employers and discounting large enterprises and the case where the employer *is* the platform vendor itself (e.g. two candidates working at Vena Solutions does not make Vena Solutions a good BD monitoring target — it has its own recruiting function and no opportunistic-agency-engagement pattern).

**AL-26.** Self-match exclusion: a candidate must never be paired against a discovered job whose hiring company matches their *current* employer. Without this, monitoring a candidate's own employer's career page can generate a match — and potentially an outreach message — telling someone about an opening at the company they already work for.

**AL-27.** New field on `company`: `engagement_scope` (`blanket | per_req | unknown`), defaulting to `unknown`, **set only by explicit human action, never inferred**. Governs whether a discovered, undisclosed second role at an already-engaged client can be approached directly (`blanket`) or requires a "confirm engagement basis" Decision first (`per_req` or `unknown`). This must never be upgraded by inference from relationship tone, history length, or placement count — only an explicit operator confirmation sets it to `blanket`.

**AL-28.** Relationship warmth must be tracked per named contact, not only at the company level. A warm relationship with one hiring manager does not imply warmth with a different, unconnected hiring manager at the same company for a different discovered role. When the company is warm but the specific new contact is cold, the correct motion is a *bridge introduction* through the known contact ("can you point me to whoever owns that req?") — a distinct third mode between cold-anonymized outreach and fully warm direct approach.

**AL-29.** Composition/ordering rule between BD's two discovery paths: whenever a company's `relationship_state` is `active_client` or further along, the warm known-client path (AL-27/AL-28) always takes precedence over the cold monitoring-and-anonymized-teaser path — regardless of whether the discovery was triggered by a pool candidate happening to work there. These are not independent, potentially-conflicting triggers; one always defers to the other based on relationship state.

## Sourcing (new sub-feature of Research Gateway, amends 02-Feature-6 and 02-Feature-11)

**AL-30.** New Decision trigger: when a job has no candidate in the pool that is both strong *and* contactable (e.g. best matches are uncontactable for lack of identity signals, or excluded on a hard filter), raise a Decision proactively — *"no strong, reachable candidate found — consider sourcing?"* — rather than relying on the operator to notice the pattern manually.

**AL-31.** Sourcing leads require a cheap relevance pre-filter before reaching full multi-agent extraction — the same lazy-tiering principle already used for fit scoring (coarse before expensive), applied one stage earlier. Raw X-ray/web search results are dominated by irrelevant noise (recruiter posts, keyword-only matches, wrong seniority) that should never reach the expensive extraction pipeline.

**AL-32.** Precise timing rule for sourced candidates: the `data_subject_record` (lawful basis, Art. 14 notification clock) is created the moment real claims are staged about a real, identified person — at workspace commit, not at the point a human later approves pursuing that lead. GDPR obligations attach to processing, not to human sign-off on processing.

**AL-33.** Sourcing campaigns need an explicit, bounded budget and a defined stopping/success condition (candidates found, spend ceiling, or time window) — analogous to the existing bound on single-candidate enrichment (max 2 rounds, budget-gated), currently missing at the campaign level. An unbounded "find good candidates for this role" campaign can spend without limit.

**AL-34.** Cold sourced outreach — first contact with someone who has never engaged with us and does not know we exist — must be its own distinct action type on the autonomy ladder (01 §I2 / 02-Feature-10), never sharing promotion history with warm nurture touches or replies to inbound applicants. A strong track record on low-risk warm messaging must not silently promote autonomy on the highest-risk outreach category.

**AL-35.** Lightweight "seen and dismissed" memory for sourcing leads that were pre-filtered out before becoming full candidate entities: a hashed-identifier + dismissal-reason + date record, distinct from and lighter-weight than the full not-same-constraint/terminal-pair memory (which requires a real candidate entity to exist). Prevents repeat campaigns from re-discovering and re-evaluating the same irrelevant hits from scratch.

**AL-36.** New reusable entity: **vertical cluster** — a named, persistent group of peer/competitor companies (schema: `company_cluster {id, org_id, name, member_company_ids[], created_via, refreshed_at}`), the company-side equivalent of candidate `segment` (01 §D5). Populated by targeted research (e.g. "UK home-heating/heat-pump software startups"), grown incrementally across successive sourcing campaigns rather than rebuilt each time, and consumed by *two* features: sourcing (Boolean search-string construction) and BD career-page monitoring (which companies to watch for new postings). One piece of infrastructure serving both.

**AL-37.** New reusable entity: **geographic catchment reference** — a named commute-time/travel-radius definition anchored to an actual office location (not a city-name string), e.g. "commutable to Waterloo station, 3 days/week." Built once per office location and reused across every job posted from that location, rather than re-reasoned per job.

**AL-38.** Explicit query-planner guidance for the "corporate-to-startup boomerang" search pattern: target candidates currently at large/established companies in the relevant domain whose career history also contains a startup/scale-up stint — a compound, two-part targeting rule (current employer type + historical employer type), not a single-attribute filter.

**AL-39.** Explicit design principle, stated as a standing rule for all future sourcing work: **sourcing-time discovery heuristics and scoring-time decision features are different risk tiers.** A signal permanently excluded from scoring (e.g. university prestige, per Feature 7's sanitized embedding projection) can still be a legitimate, *non-exclusive* input to sourcing recall — provided it never gates anyone out and never re-enters scoring once they're found. Widening who gets discovered is lower-stakes than deciding who ranks or is excluded.

## New standing guardrail (amends 01 §E invariants and §F/§G exclusions)

**AL-40.** **The system must never infer, weight, or act on a candidate-evaluation or sourcing-priority preference based on gender or any other protected characteristic — including inferring a client's presumed preference from indirect signals such as founder identity.** This applies symmetrically regardless of which direction such an inferred preference would point. It is never something the platform infers or auto-applies; it is actionable only via an explicit, legally-reviewed client instruction under a recognized lawful positive-action provision, which is out of scope for the platform itself to construct or apply. State alongside the existing "no personality/values/vibes claims" exclusion in 01 §F as a standing, non-negotiable boundary — this is the one place in the entire post-v4 testing round where a proposed capability was rejected outright rather than refined.

## Round 2 — same-batch identity collision, transcripts, and job-side compensation (amends Features 1, 2, 3, 8)

Every item below was checked for build cost before inclusion, not just usefulness — one proposal (AL-43) was dropped on exactly that basis. All are schema fields, lookup tables, or threshold/comparison rules; none require new algorithms.

**AL-41.** Same-first-name batch-collision warning. During identity resolution, if a batch contains multiple documents whose extracted candidate names share a first name with no deterministic match between them (no shared email/phone/LinkedIn), raise a Decision proactively — *"multiple documents in this batch reference an '[X]' — confirmed as separate individuals; review if this seems wrong."* Cheap: a set-intersection on names, run inside the identity-resolution step that already executes per document (Feature 2). Protects against a human, not the algorithm, conflating two people who happen to share a common first name in the same upload — confirmed as a real, non-hypothetical risk in testing (two unrelated candidates named Ioannis, correctly kept separate by the design, but only inspectable, not proactively flagged).

**AL-42.** Escalated distrust for single-mention transcript entities. Extend AL-04 (intra-document ASR variant clustering): any entity extracted from a transcript that appears **exactly once**, with no corroboration elsewhere in the same document or any other source, gets reduced confidence and routes to individual review, flagged as a possible ASR artifact. This is a count-and-threshold rule, not ASR error-correction — it doesn't try to detect *what* the model misheard, only that an uncorroborated single mention of a real, coherent entity name is exactly the shape a transcription error takes (a real company name substituted for an unrelated word, distinct from and more dangerous than the multi-variant-mangling case AL-04 already covers, since the erroneous output is indistinguishable from a genuine mention without this check).

**AL-43 — retracted.** Proposed: automatically boosting a claim's trust when a candidate's live self-correction revises it *downward* (against their own apparent interest). Retracted because it doesn't need a new mechanism: the case is already handled by existing reconciliation (a fresher, direct candidate self-report already outranks an older CV claim under the standing authority × recency rule). Detecting "is this correction self-deprecating" would require a value judgment about which claim value is more favorable — genuinely harder to build cleanly than the value it adds, since the underlying reconciliation already produces the correct outcome without it. The insight remains true and useful for a human reading the Brief; it just doesn't need to be automated.

**AL-44.** `CompensationExpectationClaim` and job-side compensation gain an explicit `currency` field, nullable/`UNKNOWN` when not stated in the source. Trivial schema addition — cross-border calls (a Brazilian candidate, a Berlin location, a UK-headquartered recruiter, a European client) routinely leave currency unstated, and a silent default is worse than an honest unknown.

**AL-45.** New claim type: `CompensationBandClaim`, scoped to the **job** entity, not the candidate — `{range_low, range_high, sweet_spot_low, sweet_spot_high, currency, source_authority}`. Populated from multiple observations across candidate calls for the same job and reconciled with the existing stint-claim-style natural-key/observation machinery (Feature 3), just pointed at a job instead of a candidate. Scope disambiguation (is a stated figure the candidate's own ask, or the recruiter describing the role's actual band?) is **speaker-attribution, not language understanding** — WEBVTT transcripts carry speaker labels, so whoever states the number determines the scope mechanically. Confirmed valuable in testing: three independent data points against one job (two candidate asks, one recruiter-stated band) converged into a single, immediately useful reference the next candidate's expectation can be checked against automatically.

**AL-46.** Geographic mismatch severity is tiered, not binary: `same_country | same_continent | intercontinental`, computed from a static country/continent reference table joining candidate location and job location — not real travel-time modeling (that remains the separate, heavier "commute catchment" concept from AL-37, reserved for same-country/same-city cases specifically). Confirmed necessary in testing: a UK-to-Canada job mismatch and a Cambridge-to-London commute were being treated identically by the design as it stood, when they are different orders of question — the Brief should say so.

**AL-47.** New flag (register per AL-13): `possible_progress_evidence_mismatch` — raised when a candidate-job pair's pipeline state has advanced significantly (past initial contact) while the candidate's profile completeness/score coverage remains very low. A comparison of two values the system already computes (`candidate_job.pair_state`, `score.coverage`), not new inference. Confirmed necessary in testing: a candidate can be deep in an active process — compensation negotiated, interview logistics settled — with literally no CV on file and no extractable skill claims, and nothing currently surfaces that gap prominently.

**AL-48.** Near-empty or failed-call transcripts need two small, mechanical accommodations: (a) a duration-vs-content-density threshold below which a transcript is treated as a failed/aborted call rather than as informative about the candidate — it must not be read as a completeness or quality signal about the person; (b) numbered transcript filenames for the same candidate and approximate date (`Transcript`, `Transcript (2)`) are recognized as retry/continuation attempts of one session via filename-suffix pattern matching, not as unrelated separate documents. Both are threshold and pattern-matching rules, not inference.

## Principles crystallized this round

Two new standing principles, promoted to the same status as P1–P12 in 00-README:

- **P13 — Sourcing:** discovery and decision are different risk tiers. A signal barred from scoring can still be a legitimate, non-exclusive input to who gets found, provided it never gates out and never leaks into scoring. *(Formalizes AL-39.)*
- **P14 — Protected characteristics:** never inferred, never weighted, never acted on absent an explicit, legally-reviewed client instruction — symmetrically, regardless of which direction a presumed preference would point. *(Formalizes AL-40.)*

## New eval golden-set cases from this round

Representative additions to 03 §1, not exhaustive: an agency-anonymized JD later resolved by a transcript (AL-05, AL-23); an ATS-export document with a named non-candidate actor in metadata (AL-16); a multi-subject shortlist PDF exercising the splitter for the first time (AL-17); a compound visa/relocation/residence JD case that must not auto-exclude an eligible-but-relocating candidate (AL-22); a sourcing campaign hitting its budget/stopping bound (AL-33); a self-match case that must be excluded (AL-26); and a deliberately-included test that a client-founder-gender signal produces **no** change in candidate ranking or sourcing priority (AL-40) — a negative case, proving the guardrail holds rather than merely asserting it.

Round 2 additions: two same-first-name candidates in one batch, with no deterministic overlap, must be kept separate **and** trigger the batch-collision Decision (AL-41); a transcript containing a single, uncorroborated real-company mention must route to review rather than silently becoming a claim (AL-42); three compensation observations against one job (two candidate-scoped, one job-scoped, disambiguated purely by speaker label) must reconcile into one `CompensationBandClaim` (AL-45); a UK-to-Canada job/candidate pairing and a same-country commute case must receive different severity tiers, not the same flag (AL-46); a candidate at an advanced pipeline state with zero extracted claims must raise `possible_progress_evidence_mismatch` (AL-47); and a near-empty, technically-failed call transcript must not lower a candidate's apparent completeness score (AL-48).

## Items checked and confirmed sound — no change needed

For completeness, per the same discipline as 03 §7: client-scoped rejection memory (Feature 8) was checked against the known-client-expansion BD scenario and correctly blocks resubmission without modification. Decision-value ranking (Feature 4) was checked against a stalled engagement-scope confirmation and correctly surfaces it via existing unblocking-effect weighting, without new ranking logic. Snapshot-based evidence handling (Feature 6) was checked against research-extractor-processed sourcing leads and requires no change — a discovered lead's web presence becomes an ordinary document exactly as designed. Round 2: the existing authority × recency reconciliation rule (B3) was checked against a candidate's live downward self-correction and found to already produce the correct outcome without modification — the basis for retracting AL-43.
